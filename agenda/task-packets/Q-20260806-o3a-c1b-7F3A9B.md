## Untrusted manager-side leads (to attack or refute, NOT premises)

1. E1 collapses to ONE inequality when beta = b0: h(a0) = g1^{-1}(b0) - b0 and
   h(b0) = g1(b0) - b0 have the same sign because g1 is increasing, so E1 is
   equivalent to g1(b0) > b0. Moreover (b0,b0) is on Gamma_2 for EVERY R (zero-width
   barrier: R2(b0,b0) = f_0(b0) = 0), and (a0,a0) is on Gamma_1 for every R. The
   sign of g1(b0) - b0 is a second-order perturbation statement at the degenerate
   corner (b0,b0); check whether a clean expansion in R - 1 or in the width is
   possible.
2. Critical points of D = lambda_2 - lambda_1 in the barrier family are exactly the
   good roots (P1 + R5). Off-diagonal critical points come in sigma-pairs of equal
   Morse index (D o sigma = D). The Poincare-Hopf index sum over the triangle (with
   boundary D = 3pi^2 on a = b) does not by itself force uniqueness, but any
   additional fact excluding off-diagonal index -1 critical points (saddles) or
   pairing constraints may close the argument.
3. Closed forms available: R1 = sin^2(s1 a)/n1 - sin^2(s2 a)/n2, and R2, and the
   secular equation sec(s;a,b,R) = 0 are all elementary trig functions of (s1,s2,a,b)
   (see c1_lib.py). The branch slopes g1' = A/B with A,B explicit implicit partials;
   a closed-form study of g1' - 1 and of h' = g1'(a) - 1/g1'(u(a)) is the natural
   Route-B continuation. Known facts: g1'(fp) -> sqrt(2), h'(fp) -> sqrt(2)-1/sqrt(2)
   as R -> inf (numerics); g1' dips below 1 near a ~ 0.42-0.44 for R >= ~1000.
4. M-shape thresholds: for R <= ~1350, h' > 0 on all of I (h strictly increasing);
   for R >= ~3000, h' < 0 on two intervals around fp with |h'| <= 0.012. A uniform-in-R
   proof might split into regimes with explicit bounds; or prove h has a single
   crossing by a different functional (e.g., J(a) = 1 - g1(1-g1(a)) fixed points and
   their derivative structure near the crossing).
5. R -> inf asymptotics: h(a0) ~ -0.38/sqrt(R), h(b0) ~ +0.38/sqrt(R) (numerical
   fits; constants unproved). The point-mass limit (barrier -> point mass at 1/2)
   may give exact asymptotics; combined with the proved R = 1 base (P4) and a
   continuity argument in R (with uniform bounds) it might prove E1 for all R.
