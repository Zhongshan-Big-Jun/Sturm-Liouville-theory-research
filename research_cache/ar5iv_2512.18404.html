Abstract.

For the classical Sturm-Liouville operators, we prove the sharp bounds for all nodes of eigenfunctions by regarding these nodes as nonlinear functionals of potential q∈L1​[0,1]q\in L^{1}[0,1]. By studying the optimization problems to minimize or to maximize the nodes {Ti,m}\{T_{i,m}\} subject to the constraint ‖q‖1=r\|q\|_{1}=r with r>0r>0 and using the strong continuity of the nodes in potentials, we obtain the explicit expressions for the sharp bounds, which are given as elementary functions.

1. Introduction

The study of Sturm-Liouville problems was originally started from the seminal papers [27, 28, 29] by Sturm and Liouville in the 1830’s and now it has become an important and wide branch in the theory of differential equations. Since the number of the literature is vast, here we only refer the reader to [22] for the historical account and the monographs [4, 32, 33] for detailed discussions. Let us consider the classical Sturm-Liouville

(1.1) −y′′+q​(x)​y=λ​y,-y^{\prime\prime}+q(x)y=\lambda y,

with Dirichlet boundary condition

(1.2) y​(0)=y​(1)=0,y(0)=y(1)=0,

where q∈ℒ1:=L1​[0,1]q\in\mathcal{L}^{1}:=L^{1}[0,1] is an integrable real potential. It follows from the classical spectral theory [4, 32, 33] that problem (1.1)-(1.2) admits a sequence of real eigenvalues λm=λm​(q)\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{m}(q) such that

λ0<λ1<⋯<λm<⋯,λm→∞(m→∞),\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}<\cdots<\lambda_{m}<\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{m}\to\infty\quad(m\to\infty),

and for each λm\lambda_{m}, there exists a corresponding real eigenfunction

Em​(x)=Em​(x;q),m=0,1,2,⋯.E_{m}(x)=E_{m}(x;q),\qquad m=0,1,2,\cdots.

Without loss of generality, we normalize the eigenfunction Em​(x)E_{m}(x) such that

Em′​(0)>0,∫01Em2​(x)​𝑑x=1.E^{\prime}_{m}(0)>0,\qquad\int_{0}^{1}E_{m}^{2}(x)dx=1.

It is well-known that E0​(x)E_{0}(x) has no zero in (0,1)(0,1) and for m∈ℕ={1,2,⋯}m\in{\mathbb{N}}=\{1,2,\cdots\}, Em​(x)E_{m}(x) has precisely mm zeros

0<T1,m<T2,m<⋯<Tm,m<1,0<T_{1,m}<T_{2,m}<\cdots<T_{m,m}<1,

which are called the nodes of problem (1.1)-(1.2). Note that the eigenvalues λm=λm​(q)\lambda_{m}=\lambda_{m}(q), the eigenfunctions Em​(x)=Em​(x;q)E_{m}(x)=E_{m}(x;q) and the nodes Ti,m=Ti,m​(q)T_{i,m}=T_{i,m}(q) are determined by the potential qq in an implicit way. Such nodes {Ti,m}\left\{T_{i,m}\right\} are significant physical quantities. For example, in vibration dynamics, a node represents a position in the system where no vibration occurs. See [3, 16, 17, 26] for qualitative properties of nodes for second order ordinary differential operators. Moreover, the information of these nodes is important in the inverse spectral theory. See for example [8, 18, 19, 23].

The aim of this paper is to find the explicit sharp upper and lower bounds for all nodes of problem (1.1)-(1.2) for given L1L^{1} norm of the potential q∈ℒ1q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}. There are already many results on sharp bounds for eigenvalues, eigenvalues ratio or eigenvalues gap for different kinds of differential operators in the literature, see for example [1, 2, 6, 9, 10, 13, 14, 20, 30, 34]. Such optimization problems have been attractive and interesting in mathematical analysis, especially in spectral theory and the theory of differential equations. Such a topic was originally started from the pioneering work of Krein [21], where he studied the sharp estimates for Dirichlet eigenvalues of

y′′+λ​q​(x)​y=0y^{\prime\prime}+\lambda q(x)y=0

by restricting that q∈L∞​[0,1],0≤q≤h,∫01q​(x)​𝑑x=rq\in L^{\infty}[0,1],0\leq q\leq h,\int_{0}^{1}q(x)dx=r with 0<r≤h.0<r\leq h.

However, as far as we know, up to now there are only few results [5, 7] on sharp bounds of nodes for ordinary differential operators. For given LpL^{p} norms of the potentials q∈Lp​[0,1]q\in L^{p}[0,1] with 1<p<∞1<p<\infty, it was obtained in a recent work [7] the optimal characterizations of locations for all nodes of problem (1.1)-(1.2). In particular, by regarding the iith node of the mmth eigenfunction as a functional of the potential, the critical equations to determine the minimizing potential are deduced so that the node is minimized, and from the critical equations, two equivalent characterizations as nonlinear systems for 44-dimensional or 22-dimensional parameters have been proved. Theoretically speaking, by taking the limit p↓1p\downarrow 1 of the results in [7], we can obtain the results on sharp bounds for the nodes when the L1L^{1} norms of the potentials are given. However, it is very difficult because the explicit expressions of the sharp bounds cannot be obtained in [7] and the results in [7] contain several singular integrals. Besides, we are not able to obtain the critical equations when the L1L^{1}-norms of the potentials are fixed, because the space ℒ1\mathcal{L}^{1} lacks local compactness even in the weak topology. For these reasons, in this paper we aim to develop a different method to work out the explicit expressions for the sharp bounds of all nodes.

Now we state our optimization problems. It is obvious that the solutions of (1.3) and (1.4) can lead to the following optimal estimates on the locations of nodes

𝐦i,m​(‖q‖1)≤Ti,m​(q)≤1−𝐦m−i+1,m​(‖q‖1),q∈ℒ1.{\bf m}_{i,m}(\|q\|_{1})\leq T_{i,m}(q)\leq 1-{\bf m}_{m-i+1,m}(\|q\|_{1}),\quad q\in{\mathcal{L}}^{1}.

However, it is difficult to solve the optimization problem (1.3). To achieve our aim and to obtain the sharp bounds for all nodes of problem (1.1)-(1.2), we need three known results on the eigenvalues {λm}\{\lambda_{m}\} and related eigenfunctions {Em​(x)}\{E_{m}(x)\}. The first known result is related to optimal estimates of eigenvalues {λm}\{\lambda_{m}\}, proved by Wei, Meng and Zhang in [30, Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.5], which will be stated as Lemma 2.5. The second known result is about a strong continuity of {λm}\{\lambda_{m}\} and the nodes {Ti,m}\left\{T_{i,m}\right\} related to the potentials. That is, the functionals

are continuous, where w1w_{1} is the topology of weak convergence in the space (ℒ1,∥⋅∥1)(\mathcal{L}^{1},\|\cdot\|_{1}). Thirdly, some basic facts of measure differential equations are needed because the solution of the related optimization problems (1.3) and (1.4) will lead to more general distributions of potentials which have no densities with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and thus it is a very natural way to understand such problems in the setting of measure differential equations. All of the above three known results will be presented as preliminary results in Section 2. In Section 3, we first trickily reduce the infinite-dimensional minimization problem (1.3) to a finite-dimensional optimization problem (3.28). Then we obtain the solutions of the finite-dimensional minimization problem (3.28) by delicate analysis, and finally we show that the solutions for the minimization problem (3.28) are just those for our minimization problem (1.3), based on the strong continuity of the nodes {Ti,m}\left\{T_{i,m}\right\} related to the potentials.
