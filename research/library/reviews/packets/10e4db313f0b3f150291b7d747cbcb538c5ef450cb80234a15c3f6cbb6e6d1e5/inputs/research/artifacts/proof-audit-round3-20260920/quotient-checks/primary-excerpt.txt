177:                        zero element for this inner product. To avoid the awkwardness of considering equivalence
178:                        classes of functions, we shift the spectrum (7) by c > 0. This allows for a left-definite

287:                        poses of this paper. Definition 1 below, taken from Definitions 2.1 and 3.1 in [6], is motivated
288:                        by five common features observed in examples listed in the above-mentioned papers.
289:                              Suppose V is a real or complex vector space and (·, ·) is an inner product on V × V such
290:                        that H = (V, (·, ·)) is a separable Hilbert space. In addition, suppose A : D( A) ⊂ H → H
291:                        is self-adjoint and bounded below by kI for some positive constant k; that is, the inequality
292:                        in (14) is satisfied. From the Hilbert space spectral theorem, for each r > 0, Ar is self-adjoint
293:                        and bounded below by kr I.
294: 
295:                        Definition 1. Let r > 0; suppose Vr is a subspace of V, and (·, ·)r is an inner product on Vr × Vr .
296:                        Let Hr = (Vr , (·, ·)r ). We say that Hr is an r th left-definite space associated with ( H, A) if each of
297:                        the following properties are satisfied:
298:                        (1)   Hr is a Hilbert space;
299:                        (2)   D( Ar ) is a subspace of Vr ;
300:                        (3)   D( Ar ) is dense in Hr ;
301:                        (4)   ( x, x )r ≥ kr ( x, x ) for x ∈ Vr ;
302:                        (5)   ( x, y)r = ( Ar x, y) for x ∈ D( Ar ) and y ∈ Vr .
