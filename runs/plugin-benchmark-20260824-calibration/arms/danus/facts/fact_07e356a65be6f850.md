# F10: Main theorem: exactly 2n simple zeros of G in (0,pi)

## STATEMENT
For every integer n>=1 and every R>1 (s=sqrt R>1), G_{n,s}(y) has exactly 2n zeros in (0,pi), all simple. Endpoint zeros at y=0 and y=pi (from the sin factor) are not counted.

## PROOF / JUSTIFICATION
By F5, G_n(y)=sin y Q_n(cos y) for all y (the identity is polynomial and valid at endpoints by continuity). On (0,pi), sin y>0 and cos y is a bijection (0,pi)->(-1,1). By F6, Q_n is even of degree 2n; by F7 and F9, Q_n has exactly n positive roots in (delta,1) (all simple), no roots in (0,delta], and by evenness exactly n roots in (-1,-delta). Hence Q_n has exactly 2n simple roots in (-1,1). Each maps to exactly one y in (0,pi); simplicity transfers through the nonzero factor sin y. Since degree Q_n=2n, these are all roots. At endpoints sin y=0 gives G=0; these are excluded.
